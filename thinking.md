# Thinking Record

## Question and aim

What counts as “home,” how does navigation differ between familiar and unfamiliar locations, and which mechanism is most important in a dense urban environment?

## Ideas, findings, and sources

### Evidence

Pigeon homing appears to use a flexible map-and-compass process involving learned landmarks, a sun compass, olfactory information, magnetic information, memory, and sometimes flockmates. Olfactory navigation is supported rather than debunked.

### Guesses

Infrared vision, polarized light, and infrasound may contribute, but the evidence is limited or mixed. Urban structures may either assist landmark recognition or create confusion.

## Decisions

Reasons for movement should be separated from mechanisms for finding home. We must also distinguish feral NYC pigeons from trained homing pigeons.

The first interactive demonstration will follow a trained homing pigeon returning to a familiar, pigeon-safe rooftop loft in NYC. The loft is a simplified fictional setting, not a claim about a particular NYC location. A later version could compare this with a feral pigeon returning to a nest or roost.

## Uncertainties and next investigation

What counts as “home,” how does navigation differ between familiar and unfamiliar locations, and which mechanism is most important in a dense urban environment?
